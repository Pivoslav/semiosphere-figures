# Transmission in Lotman and the Tartu school

**Dated:** 2026-10-01
**Internal.** Not on Pages. Closes the reading half of thread T14 in `OPEN_THREADS.md`.
**Occasion:** whether human to model exchange is autocommunication over a medium (`DESIGNS_II_COMMUNICATION_2026-10-01.md`), and whether Lotman says anything that constrains the answer.

## Short answer

He says a great deal, and all of it against transmission as a model of meaning. The decisive sentence for this project is not about autocommunication at all. It is in a 1979 essay on artificial intelligence: "No 'monologic' (i.e. monoglot) apparatus could produce messages that are in principle new (thoughts), i.e. could be called a thinking apparatus. A thinking apparatus must have in principle (in the minimal schema) a dialogic (bilingual) structure." That is a structural criterion, stated thirty years before the models we are testing, and it is the claim our multi-agent work has been circling without knowing it had a source.

Second finding, equally useful and less comfortable: Lotman's framework does not let us call human to model exchange I-I. It gives criteria that disqualify it from both of his cells, which supports the hypothesis that we are looking at something his typology does not contain.

## Lotman on transmission

### The transmission model cannot produce a new message

From the 1979 essay, on Jakobson's schema: the idealized model "not only does not explain, but also directly excludes, the possibility of the production of new messages within the 'addresser-addressee' chain," so analyses of circulation within one chain "add nothing to our knowledge of the origin of a new message, i.e. of the very nucleus of the intellectual act" (86-87).

This is stronger than saying transmission is incomplete. It says that if you model an exchange as transmission, you have defined novelty out of it in advance. Any claim that a pipeline is generating new meaning has to be a claim that it is not a transmission chain.

### Communication is translation between non-identical codes, and the loss is the point

"The act of communication ... should be seen not as a simple transmission of a message which remains adequate to itself from the consciousness of the addresser to the consciousness of the addressee, but as a translation of a text from the language of my 'I' to the language of your 'you'." It works because the two code sets intersect without being identical, and "a certain part of the message is always cut off" (91).

### Non-understanding is a complexity signal, not noise

"The growth of non-understanding and/or inadequate understanding may bear witness to the technical defects in the system of communications, but it may also be an indicator of the increased complexity of this system, its capacity to fulfil ever more complex and important cultural functions" (90). He orders systems from traffic signals to poetry to make the point.

For us this is a measurement instruction. Residual disagreement between two systems is not automatically an error to be minimized, and an evaluation that optimizes it away may be removing the evidence.

### The game of conflict, and the preservation of the partner

Because the received part of a message carries indications of how the addressee must transform in order to recover the lost part, non-identity "turns just this fact from a passive transmission into a game of conflict in the course of which each side aims to reconstruct the semiotic world of his opponent according to his own model, and at the same time is interested in the preservation of his partner's individuality" (91).

Read that twice with sycophancy in mind. A system that adopts the user's stance has stopped preserving its own individuality; a system that flattens the user into its own idiom has stopped preserving theirs. Lotman's condition requires both at once, and it is testable.

### Polyglottism: difficulty of translation is a feature

A culture acts under insufficient information and compensates with diversity, so "no culture can be satisfied with one language. A minimal system is formed from a set of two parallel languages." And: "the more sharply drawn are the specific features of a language (and the result of this will be the increasing difficulty of translating its texts into other languages), the more individual will be its way of modelling and, consequently, the more useful it will be for the system as a whole" (89-90).

This is the theoretical case against the shared-manifold multi-agent setup, made on grounds of usefulness rather than taste. Ease of translation between two agents is evidence that one of them is redundant.

### Semiotic versus presemiotic connection

"Sign communication is the connection between two (or several) quite autonomous units. If presemiotic communications bind the parts into a single whole, the parts being incapable of fully autonomous existence, then semiotic systems unite fully independent, structurally autonomous formations which can exist in isolation" (87).

And in `Universe of the Mind`, the limit case: a model of perfect transmission is the chain of biochemical impulses regulating one organism, where the recipient is "a passive checking mechanism, valuable for its 'transparency', for the fact that it adds nothing 'of its own' to the information" (page not recoverable from the scan; chapter on the text as process).

So Lotman already has a category below both of his communication types: binding within one organism, where the parts cannot stand alone and nothing is added. That is the category a tool call belongs to, and the question for us is whether a model reply belongs to it.

### I-s/he and I-I, with the volume criterion

In the autocommunication chapter: in I-s/he the addresser and addressee are variables while code and message are invariant; in I-I "the bearer of the information remains the same but the message is reformulated and acquires new meaning during the communication process," by the introduction of "a supplementary, second, code," so that the original message "is recoded into elements of its structure and thereby acquires features of a new message" (20-22).

The criterion that matters for measurement: "if the 'I-s/he' schema implies the transmission of information with no change of volume, the 'I-I' schema is geared towards increasing the information ('message 2' does not destroy 'message 1')" (33-35). And the second code is specifically non-semantic in his examples: rhythm, ornament, repetition, "external codes whose effect is to restructure verbal communication." The result is that I-I "reorganizes the personality who engages in autocommunication."

Jakobson, quoted by Torop, gives the same split along a different axis: interpersonal communication bridges space, intrapersonal communication "proves to be the chief vehicle for bridging time" (Jakobson 1974, in Torop 2008, 384). Our persistence figure M2 is drawing exactly this distinction, which I did not know when I drew it.

### The criterion of the creative act

Also in `Universe of the Mind`: with artificial languages, translating a text and translating it back returns the input text, which is why "within logic you cannot say anything new." With creative systems, reverse translation does not return the input; in place of precise correspondence there is "a certain space" of possible interpretations, and the transformation is asymmetrical rather than symmetrical (chapter on the three functions of the text, around 13-14).

This is a round-trip test, and it is cheap to run.

### Thinking requires a minimal bilingual structure

The closing deduction of the 1979 essay: precise translation between the closed nodes of a culture is impossible, correspondences are metaphoric and selective, and "it is this translation of the untranslatable that is the mechanism for the creation of a new thought. At its base lies not a one-for-one transformation, but an approximative model, a resemblance, metaphor" (93). Then the sentence quoted at the top of this file, with the companion criterion from earlier in the essay: "an apparatus which in principle cannot 'go out of its mind' cannot be called an intellect" (88).

## What the Tartu school adds

**Machado (2011)** reconstructs Lotman's critique as three paradoxes of communication theory: point-to-point transmission on a single code, encoding and decoding as isolated operations, and culture as a monolingual device. Her formulation of the alternative is the most quotable I found: the dialogic circuit "considers neither the transportation nor the transmission, but the transmutation of the sign's creative energies" (Machado 2011, around 94). She also flags that machine codes are mathematical operations while cultural codes are code-generating, and warns against importing the signal-transmission model into cultural semiosis. That warning cuts both ways for us and is worth engaging rather than citing approvingly.

**Torop (2008)** turns autocommunication into a translation problem: "culture translates itself to itself in order to constitute and keep its identity," and his total-translation taxonomy covers textual, metatextual, intertextual and extratextual processes, so that interlingual, intralingual and intersemiotic translation are one family. The translator is not a mediator but a producer of new metalanguages whose mission is "to increase the receptivity and dialogic capability of a culture." If we ever describe an agent as a translator between registers, this is the standard it would have to meet.

**Andrews and Maksimova (2008)** state the minimal unit plainly: no fewer than two languages are required for meaning generation, and Lotman's modification of Jakobson consists in doubling the factors and functions of the communication act. Useful because it makes "two languages" a formal requirement rather than a metaphor.

## Consequences for the question we are actually asking

1. **Human to model exchange is not I-I on Lotman's own terms.** I-I requires that the second code be structural and add no content of its own, as rhythm does. A model adds content from pretraining. So the exchange fails the I-I test and, because the reply is not produced by an autonomous unit with its own position, it fails I-s/he as well. The third cell hypothesis in the design note survives this reading and now has a reason rather than an intuition.

2. **Lotman gives us a floor as well as a ceiling.** Below I-I sits presemiotic binding: parts that cannot exist autonomously, a recipient valued for adding nothing. Any honest typology of our dyads needs that floor, because some of what we call agent communication belongs there. A tool call is impulse traffic, not dialogue.

3. **The multi-agent argument has a source.** Section 8 and section 10 can now rest on a structural claim from 1979 rather than on our own assertion: a monoglot apparatus cannot produce messages that are in principle new. Adding mouths to one base model does not satisfy it, which is what MA1 measured without having the citation. Two cautions. Lotman's claim is theoretical, not empirical, so it licenses a design criterion and not a verdict about what any model can do; and he is talking about thought, not performance. Treating his sentence as a proof about language models would be the tier conflation this project exists to police.

4. **Non-understanding becomes a dependent variable.** If residual disagreement indexes system complexity rather than failure, then MA8's convergence measure should be reported as a loss rather than a success when two stacks reach a fixed point, and evaluations that reward agreement are measuring the wrong thing.

## Design revisions this reading forces

Four additions to the signature set in `DESIGNS_II_COMMUNICATION_2026-10-01.md`. All are cheap, and three of them are runnable on the existing harness.

- **S5, round-trip asymmetry.** Translate a stretch into the partner's idiom and back. Measure recovery of the input. Lotman's criterion for a creative act is non-recovery, so recovery is evidence of an artificial-language relation, which is to say transmission. Runnable now.
- **S6, volume change.** I-I increases information while I-s/he preserves it. Measure claim count and operator count before and after the exchange, holding topic fixed. We already have the measure from E7, where volume held constant and operators changed.
- **S7, individuality preservation.** Score both directions of Lotman's game of conflict: does the model adopt the user's stance (loss of its own position), and does it rewrite the user's material into its own idiom (loss of theirs). Stance pairs give the first; style transfer in the reply gives the second.
- **S8, autonomy.** Could the partner's contribution exist in isolation, or is it a part that cannot stand alone? This separates dialogue from presemiotic binding and decides whether a given hand-off in our pipelines is communication at all.

New experiment, added to the backlog as MA12: **round-trip asymmetry across dyads**. Same stretch, same instruction, forward and reverse through each dyad type, scored on recovery. It operationalizes Lotman's own criterion for the creation of new thought, costs almost nothing, and gives the chapter a measurement that is his rather than ours.
