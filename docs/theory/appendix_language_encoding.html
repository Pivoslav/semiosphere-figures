Appendix · language encoding

How a sentence becomes a vector in this project, what each symbol in the equations means, and what that vector can and cannot justify.

What an encoder does

An embedding is a numeric fingerprint of a sentence: a fixed-length list of numbers. The model used here is paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (Sentence-Transformers checkpoint; Reimers and Gurevych 2019), built from a twelve-layer MiniLM encoder (Wang et al. 2021) over a multilingual transformer stack (Vaswani et al. 2017). Text is split into WordPiece tokens (Devlin et al. 2019). Weights are frozen on the Montreal corpus (not fine-tuned on your documents). Implementation: SentenceTransformer.encode in scripts/run_semiosphere_feature_space.py.

Two fingerprints that sit close together usually share topic. They do not reliably preserve register (scare quotes, gathering-not-symposium, which shell a stretch belongs to). That is why the Lotman reading treats the encoder as an instrument on the primary language layer - good at translation-equivalence, weak at institutional shell by itself.

Sentence text→ Tokens→ 12-layer transformer→ Mean pool→ 384 numbers (one vector)

Mean pooling equation

e = (1/n) Σi=1n hi   ∈ ℝ384
What this equation says

Take every token's internal vector from the last layer, add them up, divide by how many tokens there were. The result e is the sentence fingerprint stored for the probe and retriever.

SymbolStands forWhat happens to it
eSentence embeddingBuilt once per scored stretch; one row in the embedding matrix.
nToken countLonger sentences have larger n; each token counts equally in the average.
hiHidden vector for token iComes out of layer 12; already mixes context from the whole sentence.
ΣSumAdd all n token vectors component-wise (384 additions per dimension).
1/nDivide by countTurns a sum into an average so length does not automatically scale the arrow.
ℝ384384-dimensional spaceEach e is a point (arrow from origin) with 384 coordinates.
Plain-language walkthrough
  1. Tokenize the sentence into WordPiece subwords (Devlin et al. 2019).
  2. Run pieces through the transformer; keep the last layer's vector for each piece (Vaswani et al. 2017).
  3. Mean-pool token vectors into one sentence vector (Reimers and Gurevych 2019, default in Sentence-Transformers).
  4. Store the resulting e as this stretch's fingerprint.

Toy example (2 numbers instead of 384): three tokens with hidden vectors (1,0), (1,2), (3,2). Sum = (5,4). Divide by 3 → e ≈ (1.67, 1.33). One sentence, one arrow.

Cosine similarity

The retriever and probe compare fingerprints by cosine similarity: how much two arrows point the same way, not how long they are (standard vector-space measure; Manning, Raghavan, and Schütze 2008; computed via scikit-learn in the probe script).

Step 1 · Dot product

x · y = Σk=1384 xk yk
What this part does

Multiply matching coordinates and add the products. Large positive value → many coordinates push together; mixed signs → directions disagree.

SymbolMeaning
x, yTwo sentence fingerprints (query vs candidate, or secret vs outward).
xkThe kth number in vector x (one coordinate).
x · yA single score from the whole vector - not itself the cosine yet.

2D example: x = (0.8, 0.6), y = (0.6, 0.8). Dot = 0.8×0.6 + 0.6×0.8 = 0.96.

Step 2 · Length (norm)

‖x‖ = √(x · x)
What this part does

Dot the vector with itself, take the square root - Pythagoras in 384 dimensions. Norm is how long the arrow is before you compare direction.

Cosine divides by ‖x‖ and ‖y‖ so a very long vector does not automatically look "more similar."

Step 3 · Cosine (the score we report)

cos(θ) = (x · y) / (‖x‖ ‖y‖)
What happens in order
  1. Compute x · y (overlap of coordinates).
  2. Compute lengths ‖x‖ and ‖y‖.
  3. Divide: dot product divided by both lengths → a number from −1 to 1.
  4. Interpret: near 1 = same direction (often same topic); near 0 = unrelated angle; negative = opposite direction.

Before comparing secret and outward clouds in the probe, vectors pass VecMap-style preprocessing: L2 normalize, subtract the training-side mean, L2 normalize again (Artetxe, Labaka, and Agirre 2016; Artetxe, Labaka, and Agirre 2018; function artetxe_preprocess in run_semiosphere_feature_space.py). That is the centering and normalization step so cosine tracks angle cleanly. Cross-lingual maps on top of those vectors follow the Mikolov → Xing → Artetxe chain documented on Semiotic coordinate plain §5 (Mikolov, Le, and Sutskever 2013; Xing et al. 2015; orthogonal Procrustes: Schönemann 1966).

Montreal retriever: flat search returned the internal memo at cosines about 0.48-0.76 to outward-clip questions - high theme overlap, wrong shell. Scoped by shell → tier confusion 0.

Glossary · terms on this page

Dotted words in the text open a short definition; click again or use Full definition ↓ to jump here.

Encoder
Frozen neural model that reads text and outputs vectors. Here: MiniLM-L12, not fine-tuned on the archive.
Embedding
Same as the sentence fingerprint e - one vector per scored stretch.
Token
A word piece after tokenization; each gets a hidden vector hi before pooling.
Transformer
Stack of attention layers; each token's final hidden state depends on the whole sentence context.
Mean pooling
Average token vectors into one sentence vector; equation at #eq-mean-pool.
Vector
Ordered list of numbers treated as an arrow from the origin; 384 entries here.
Dot product
Sum of products of matching coordinates; measures coordinate-wise overlap before normalization.
Norm
Length of a vector, ‖x‖ = √(x · x).
Cosine similarity
Dot product divided by both lengths; equals cos(θ) between arrows. Used in retrieval and probe scores.
Centering / normalization
VecMap-style L2 → mean-center (training mean reused on test) → L2 (Artetxe, Labaka, and Agirre 2016; 2018).
Frozen
Model weights fixed; the archive is measured, not used to retrain the encoder.
Multilingual
Training objective pulls paraphrases and translations in different languages near each other.
Theme / topic
What the sentence is about (famine, film, Canada). Encoders recover this more easily than register.
Register
Document type and audience contract - secret memo vs embassy release vs diaspora weekly.
Shell
Archive typing for which zone a unit lives in (secret, inward, Soviet outward, rival).
Pair-read
Read secret stretch beside dated outward counterpart or record a documented miss - historical warrant.
Probe
Fit maps and controls on frozen embeddings; microscope for theme-level linearity, not oracle for history.
