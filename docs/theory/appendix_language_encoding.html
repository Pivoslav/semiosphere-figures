Appendix · language encoding

How a sentence becomes a vector in this project, and what that vector can and cannot justify.

What an encoder does (plain language)

An encoder (here: a frozen sentence-transformer) turns a stretch of text into a list of numbers - a embedding or fingerprint. You can think of it as coordinates in a high-dimensional space. Two sentences whose fingerprints are close are usually talking about similar topics (famine, film, Canada). They are not guaranteed to preserve register (secret memo vs embassy release vs diaspora press): scare quotes, gathering-not-symposium, and proof tier live in the archive's typing, not in the encoder's default geometry.

This dissertation uses paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2: a small multilingual model trained so that paraphrases and translations in different languages land near each other. That training choice is why section 2 of the Lotman reading treats the encoder as an instrument on the primary language layer - good at translation-equivalence, weak at institutional shell by itself.

Sentence text→ Word pieces (tokens)→ 12-layer transformer→ Mean pool over tokens→ 384 numbers (one vector)

Mean pooling (what the model outputs)

e = (1/n) Σi=1n hi   ∈ ℝ384
Plain language

Each token gets a 384-dimensional hidden vector h from the last layer. The sentence fingerprint is the average of those token vectors. One paragraph in, one arrow out - that is what the probe and the retriever store for each sign-event stretch.

n is the number of tokens (word pieces) after tokenization; longer sentences average over more pieces.

Cosine similarity (the ruler we use)

Retrieval and the semiosphere probe score candidates with cosine similarity: the angle between two fingerprint arrows, not their length.

cos(θ) = (x · y) / (‖x‖ ‖y‖)
Plain language

After centering and normalizing (Artetxe-style preprocessing in the probe script), cosine is between −1 and 1. Near 1: same general direction in topic space. Near 0: unrelated direction. The E4 retriever returned the internal memo at cosines roughly 0.48–0.76 to outward-clip questions - high theme overlap, wrong shell.

Worked example (2D sketch below): if x = (0.8, 0.6) and y = (0.6, 0.8), then x·y = 0.96, ‖x‖ = ‖y‖ = 1, so cos(θ) = 0.96 (small angle). If y = (−0.6, 0.8), cosine is 0 (perpendicular).

In a 2D cartoon of the real 384-D space, multilingual training pulls translation mates together. Register (shell) is a secondary layer the encoder does not label for you.
