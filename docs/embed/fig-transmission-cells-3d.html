Lotman's rooms, and the one he left out (T1, 3D)

Lotman sorts exchanges with two questions. Does the message change size on the way across? Does the receiver add anything of its own? His named answers are the three wire rooms. The solid room is the corner he never filled in, and the blue ball is the median of our 26 real delegations, which sits right in it: about 4.4 times the volume, with 0.84 of the report's vocabulary new.

How to read this

The floor has two axes, labelled in the scene: whether the volume changes, and how much the receiver adds. The four rooms are the four corners of that floor, the same quadrants as the 2D chart. Position inside a room is not scaled, and the rooms' heights mean nothing.

Ordinary sending, I-s/he, keeps the message the same size. Autocommunication lets it grow, but only through rhythm and repetition, with no new facts. Presemiotic binding sits beneath both: parts of one organism, where the receiver is prized for adding nothing. A subagent's report grows and fills up with new words, so it fits none of the three, and the plain transmission picture that Lotman criticises rules out new messages altogether.

ρ̃ = 4.3843,   g̃ = 0.8387,   n = 26

The measured medians for MA13. ρ is report characters over instruction characters, and g is the share of the report's vocabulary absent from the instruction. ρ̃ is far above 1, so the I-s/he test fails. g̃ is far above 0, so the I-I content test fails. The receiver plainly adds a great deal, so the presemiotic case fails too. That leaves the fourth corner.

From tests to rooms the layout

The floor is the 2D chart's two-by-two grid laid flat. Left to right is whether the volume of information changes; back to front is how much the receiver adds. Each room is the same box, 1.5 × 0.9 × 1.5 units, centred on its quadrant at (±1.8, ±1.8), and it follows the 2D canvas's placement. The blue ball sits inside the unnamed room by category. Its position inside the room is not scaled by ρ or g, and the rooms' heights mean nothing.

The 2D page's guide text calls I-s/he the "receiver adds little" corner, while its canvas places it in the top row. This figure follows the canvas until that is settled.

In plain words

What you are looking at

  • The floor is split into four rooms by two yes-or-no questions. Does the message get bigger on the way across? Does the receiver add material of its own?
  • The three wire rooms are the kinds of communication Lotman named. Ordinary sending: a letter that arrives as written. Autocommunication: someone rereading their own notes and reorganising their thinking, so the message grows but no new facts arrive. Presemiotic binding: signals between parts of one body, where the receiver adds nothing.
  • The solid room is the combination he left empty: the message gets bigger and the receiver adds a lot that is new.
  • The blue ball is where the 26 AI hand-offs from the delegation figure land.

What it shows

An AI agent handing work to a subagent fits none of Lotman's named kinds. The report comes back about four times longer, and most of its words were not in the request.

Why it matters

The dissertation uses Lotman's types to read Soviet institutions. Secret KGB memos are partly the institution talking to itself; embassy releases are ordinary sending aimed outward. To use the same tools on AI pipelines, you first need to know which room an AI hand-off belongs in, and it belongs in none of them. Either the typology needs a new room, or AI delegation is a new kind of exchange. The journal's position is to admit the room exists before naming it. That keeps the dissertation from forcing machines into categories built for people and institutions.

Potential for the bigger picture

Useful as a frame, and honest about its limits. Placing one median in a quadrant is a categorical argument, not a measured distance, and it rests on the site's own reading of Lotman's cells. It gets much stronger once other kinds of exchange are placed on the same floor and scored the same way: human-to-model sessions (MA7), sibling subagents talking through a parent (MA11 and MA16), and the archive's own memo-to-release pairs. Then the empty room is compared with something other than itself.

It would also help to measure the presemiotic and I-I corners directly rather than by description. If several different exchanges land in the unnamed room, the dissertation has a case for naming it. If only AI delegation does, the finding is narrower but still useful.
