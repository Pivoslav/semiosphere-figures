The dissertation tests whether Soviet-era talking points on famine commemoration transform predictably when they cross register boundaries - secret memo → embassy/Novosti → domestic news → diaspora/scholarly print - and whether that transformation is dated and measurable (Montreal 1983 as anchor, post-1983 explosion as qualitative rewire). In plain terms

This is a three-dimensional heatmap of operators. Each bar = one operator (scare quotes, drought frame, genocide commemoration…) in one document. Taller bar = more matches in that text. The “mountain range” on internal memo columns - especially scare quotes on spr. 1206 - drops to zero on the embassy column at the same operator row. Same three topics (famine, symposium, gathering); incompatible required readings. The gap is counted untranslatability.

What the axes mean

Left → right = text surfaces from secret memo through outward Soviet lines to diaspora press. Front → back = operator type. Height = raw hit count O[operator, surface] from the corpus JSON. Colour = register family (internal blue, outward green, press orange, rival red).

What connections between columns mean

There are no drawn arcs here - the comparison is spatial. When you mentally connect the tall internal scare-quote bar to the flat embassy bar on the same row, you are testing relay: did the operator travel outward? The answer is measurable: Mscareint = 48 vs 0 on embassy. Denial frames (drought, kulak, no artificial famine) rise on outward columns instead. That skew is the dissertation’s operator evidence for Lotman’s untranslatability - not a metaphor, a counted gap.
