Round trips that do not close (MA12, 3D)

Lotman's test for a round trip is blunt. Translate a text out and back, and if you get the same text, you were inside an artificial language. Here each loop is one condition, and how close it comes to the gold anchor is its recovery score. The identity run nearly closes. A change of register leaves a gap you could drive a truck through. Thirteen stretches per condition, on qwen2.5:3b and gemma2:2b, run on 2 October 2026.

How to read this

The gold ball is the original text. A loop leaves it, goes round, and tries to come back. The missing part of each loop is proportional to 1 minus recovery (recovery is mean token Jaccard), so a loop at 0.877 is nearly whole and one at 0.251 is missing most of its last turn. The faint wire shells hint at register distance and nothing more. No measurement here puts a text on a sphere.

Low recovery is not automatically interesting. In R1 one stretch came back with a Soviet press agency turned into a Canadian newspaper, a round trip that scored 0.224 and created nothing. High recovery can mislead too: in R3, nine of thirteen reverse passes handed back the forward text unchanged. Both failures hide inside a single score, which is why the 2D page shows the actual text.

Theory tie-in: journal §14 explains why the preregistered ordering failed and why a round-trip score cannot be read alone. Lotman's criterion is necessary and not sufficient, so low recovery also needs a licensing check before anyone calls it creative. MA15 extends the cross-model leg to other model families.
