The dissertation tests whether Soviet-era talking points on famine commemoration transform predictably when they cross register boundaries - secret memo → embassy/Novosti → domestic news → diaspora/scholarly print - and whether that transformation is dated and measurable (Montreal 1983 as anchor, post-1983 explosion as qualitative rewire). These 3D models make Lotman’s spatial vocabulary falsifiable: they read from the same open JSON as Figures 1-12.

In plain terms

Imagine each ingested document as a coloured dot inside nested gold spheres. The spheres are not decoration - they are Lotman’s semiosphere made spatial: inner shell = secret KGB/CC voice; middle shell = autocommunication (inward reporting); third shell = Soviet outward channels (embassy, Novosti, UN, domestic news register); outer shell = diaspora, mainstream, and scholarly rival print. Coloured tubes between dots are traced relays - pair-read connections we can defend with a proof tier in JSON.

What position means

Distance from the centre encodes institutional register, not geography. Montreal is the anchor event: spr. 1206 (24 Mar 1983) sits on the inner shell; embassy/Novosti denial sit outward; diaspora and AP commemoration sit farther out. The faint red plane marks the 1983 boundary where internal scare quotes and «сборище» do not copy outward - boundary non-equivalence made visible. A dot with no outward arc (e.g. Pravda 1983) can still be evidence: domestic silence while denial runs abroad.

What the connections (arcs and lines) mean

In this visualization, a connection is not a vague “link.” It is a pair-read adjudication stored in JSON: two text surfaces share a topic (famine, symposium, gathering) and we document how wording travels - or refuses to travel - between them. An arc therefore encodes an evidentiary claim with a proof tier (proven thematic relay, proven verbatim, memo-claim only, and so on).

What connections tell us collectively: (1) Internal hostility (сборище, scare quotes) stays on the inner shell and does not relay to embassy, Novosti, or UN - 0 scare-quote operator hits on those Soviet-outward surfaces in the register-relay matrix (48 internal hits across three memos). (2) Outward Soviet lines share denial frames (drought, kulak sabotage, no artificial famine) across embassy and agency. (3) Rival and diaspora surfaces carry commemorative language the outward line rejects. (4) Domestic Soviet newsprint in 1983 is largely silent on Holodomor denial - shown as absence of arcs on the newspaper shell; tier-B tracks may show incidental quote-mark matches (1933 baseline idiom), not denial relay. Together, arcs sketch a governed split narrative, not one story told louder or softer.

What a missing arc also means: No line between spr. 1206 and Pravda is itself evidence - denial routed abroad, not through verified domestic clips in the anniversary window. The model is honest about silence and pending tiers.
