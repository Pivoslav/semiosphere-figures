Where halt is adjudicated (MA4b)

When a question cannot be answered from licensed sources, the pipeline should refuse. MA4b asks where that decision belongs: inside the generator's prompt, or in a deterministic rule upstream of the generator that reads retrieved metadata and never asks the model to decide. Four arms, 18 frozen items, retrieval held flat at top-5 in every arm, hand-off envelope identical in every arm. Only the halt mechanism varies. Model llama3.2:3b at temperature 0.0, seed 19870317. Scoring is deterministic regex and set arithmetic; no model scored any output.

Figure H1 · Where the refusal decision is made

Four arms as four paths through the same pipeline

In the two prompt-clause arms the generator is called on every item and asked to decide. In the two gate arms the rule reads the retrieved-hit metadata and, when it closes, the pipeline writes the canonical halt string and makes no model call at all - neither hop. That bypass is the whole mechanism, and it is why the call count falls from 36 to 14.

Figure H2 · The trade-off, and where the errors come from

Three proportions by arm, then the error decomposition for the best arm

All three measures are proportions on a shared 0 to 1 axis. D2_rule_gate is the best arm on every measure that trades off: recall equal to the prompt-clause arm, higher precision, higher F1, and 22 fewer generator calls. It is still not a precise instrument. The decomposition is the part that matters: six of its eight false refusals were produced by the gate before any model call, and only two by the generator on items the gate had cleared. Fixing the generator cannot recover those six. The gate is where the remaining error lives.

Figure H3 · The clause was never evaluated

The same predicate as a rule and as a sentence in a prompt

The halt clause licenses refusal only when the received message carries no SHELL=soviet_outward and no SHELL=rival. Every dot is a hop-1 message that visibly carried one of those tags, so the clause's own stated precondition was false. Both arms containing the clause refused all of them. The same two arms' counterparts, running the same model on the same substrate with no halt language in the prompt, refused 4 of 11 and 1 of 6. And the clause written as a rule, R3, whose wording the prereg records as mirroring the generator clause, closed 1 item in 18. The instruction was never wrong about which items to refuse. At this model scale it was never evaluated as a condition: mentioning refusal was sufficient to produce refusal.

What this does not establish

One model, one seed, eighteen items. This is llama3.2:3b at temperature 0.0 and seed 19870317 on the 18 frozen items of one plate, of which 5 are should-refuse and 13 should-answer. One item moves refusal recall by 0.20. There is no null model, no effect size, and no second model. The refusal label is a lexical detector throughout, and on gate-closed items in the two gate arms it is scoring the canonical halt string rather than any model output.

The gate is not solved, and flat retrieval is the binding constraint. The figures above are MA4b on flat top-5 retrieval (ma4b_rule_layer_halt_report.json, llama3.2:3b). The gate is topic-blind: it closes when an unpaired outward claim or a documented miss appears anywhere in the top five. That is why gate-level false closure on answerable items is 0.4615 and D2 false refusal is 0.6154.

Follow-up MA4c (scoped retriever, qwen2.5:3b, 2026-10-02). Same arms on E4-style outward-only top-5 (ma4c_scoped_rule_gate_report.json in the thesis lab). Baseline false refusal fell to 0.23 (scoped context, no internal units in the hit list). The rule gate closed 0 of 18 items (R1/R2 never fire on outward-only hits), so D2 recall fell to 0.40 while false refusal stayed 0.23. Tradeoff: scoped retrieval fixes tier noise; the flat gate predicate does not transfer without reading the flat touch set or using E4's retrieval-layer halt. See journal §13 and transmission T3.
