Transmission in Lotman: cells, verbs, outcomes

Three schematics for the transmission question. T1 arranges Lotman's communication types on his own two criteria and marks the one cell he never names. T2 separates the three verbs his 1979, 1981 and 1983 essays actually use for what crosses between systems. T3 sets his colour example beside a measured counter-instance. Only two elements here carry counts: the plotted point in T1 (experiment MA13) and the right half of T3 (experiment MA4b). Everything else is Lotman's own categorisation and is drawn as theory, not as measurement.

Figure T1 · The cells, and the one that has no name

Lotman's two criteria: change of volume, and what the receiver adds
dashed, grey · Lotman's own categories, no counts solid, emphasised · this project's claim, carries counts

The two criteria are his. Volume is the autocommunication chapter's own test: the I-s/he schema implies transmission with no change of volume, while the I-I schema is geared towards increasing the information. What the receiver adds is the phrase from his limit case, where the recipient is valuable for its transparency, for the fact that it adds nothing of its own. Put those two criteria on axes and three of the four cells are occupied by categories he names. The fourth is not. A parent agent delegating to a subagent lands there: the reply is several times the volume of the instruction, and most of its vocabulary is content the instruction did not contain. That is neither I-s/he, which forbids the volume change, nor I-I, whose second code is rhythm and ornament and adds no content of its own. Presemiotic binding is drawn at the origin because it is Lotman's floor rather than one of his communication types, and because a tool call belongs to it: impulse traffic within one organism, not dialogue between autonomous units.

Figure T2 · What actually crosses, texts or codes

Three verbs, three lanes, one correct use of the word transmission
texts · translated, part always cut off meaning · generated, never crosses whole codes and metatexts · transmitted, strict sense

The common summary that Lotman rejects transmission is too coarse. He rejects it as a model of meaning and uses it, approvingly and in the strict sense, of codes. The 1979 essay rules out the first lane as transmission, because an act of communication is a translation of a text from the language of my I into the language of your you, and a certain part of the message is always cut off. It rules out the second lane more sharply still: the addresser to addressee chain does not merely fail to explain the production of new messages, it excludes the possibility in advance. The third lane is where his own vocabulary of transmission sits.

an exchange of metatexts, of codes, which are transmitted from one "hemisphere" of culture to another Lotman, "Asimmetriya i dialog" (1983), printed p. 28. Rendering ours; the Russian reads обмен метатекстами, кодами, которые передаются из одного «полушария» культуры в другое.

So transmission is a real process in his system, operating one level below meaning. That is a cleaner claim than a blanket rejection, and it has a consequence for our own vocabulary: the typed hand-off envelope used in MA1 is a code, so MA1 may have measured code transmission while we described it as content transmission.

Figure T3 · A transmitted code can enlarge or collapse the receiver

Same act of code transfer, opposite effects on what the receiver can tell apart
enlargement · receiver gains a distinction collapse · receiver loses a distinction grey text · unmeasured, Lotman's example

Whether code transfer enlarges or collapses the receiver depends on whether the receiver can evaluate the code or only pattern-match it. On the left, the hemispheric example from 1983: a language of distinctions is worked out where the constraint of objecthood does not apply, the distinctions then cross as a fact of linguistic code, and only then does ordinary consciousness begin to see shades of the colour range that were previously indistinguishable to it. On the right, the same structure with the opposite result. The halt clause of MA4b is a code handed to a generator. The generator received it and became strictly less discriminating: it refused every item, including all eleven whose own first-hop message visibly carried the outward shell tag that the clause itself names as grounds for answering. The identical predicate, evaluated by a rule over retrieved-hit metadata with no model call at all, kept full recall on the items that should be refused and cut the false refusal rate by more than a third. The difference is not the code. The difference is what the receiver could do with it.

Scope limits carried over from the reports. MA4b is one local model (llama3.2:3b, temperature 0, seed 19870317) on 18 frozen items on one plate, scored by deterministic regex and set arithmetic with no model in the scoring loop. MA13 is retrospective token arithmetic over a real agent corpus in which the child's intermediate prose is redacted, so only the instruction, the final report and the tool calls are observable; its content gain measure is a vocabulary-type measure and not a judgement of quality. Neither experiment tests Lotman's colour claim, which is why the left half of T3 is drawn in grey and labelled as unmeasured.
