How this figure is made

Each panel opens one step on the way from the memos to the two networks, with the equation it uses.

The count: shared memos what each line measures
wperiod(a, b) = Σm ∈ memos(period) 1[a ∈ m] · 1[b ∈ m]

For two phrase families a and b, the weight is the number of memos in a period that contain both. 1[a ∈ m] is 1 if family a appears in memo m and 0 if not. Before 1983 there are only two memos, and after 1983 there are fourteen.

new(a, b) ⇔ wafter(a, b) > 0 and wbefore(a, b) = 0

A line is red if the pair occurs together after 1983 and never before. Before 1983 the network has three topics and three lines. After it has seven topics and nineteen lines: three carried over and sixteen new. That gives the summary under the figure, Δ topics = 4 and Δ links = 16. The table under the figure lists every line with its count.

From counts to the picture planes, positions, thickness
tube radius = 0.04 + min(0.08, 0.012 · w),   y = −3 before 1983,   y = +3 after

Lines get thicker with the number of shared memos, up to a cap. The two periods sit on separate planes. Dot positions on each plane are stored in the data. The lower plane is a simple triangle, because all three early topics occur in the same early memos (1976 to 1977). On the upper plane, topics that already existed keep their left-right position and new ones are placed near the topics they co-occur with. Grey pillars join a topic's two positions. Position on a plane is a layout choice, not a measurement.

Colours: gold for lines on the lower plane, blue for lines carried over on the upper plane, red for new lines.

What the motion does the buttons under the figure

Play before and after moves the view down to the before-1983 plane, holds while the caption describes it, then rises to the after-1983 plane and settles back where it started, about fifteen seconds in all. Only the camera moves; the network is fixed. Turn the model spins the two planes slowly. Nothing starts on its own.

In plain words

What you are looking at

  • Two maps of the secret memos' vocabulary, one stacked above the other. The bottom map covers the memos written before the 1983 Montreal symposium, the top map the memos written after it.
  • Each dot is a topic or turn of phrase, such as scare quotes, references to Ukrainian nationalist groups, or calling the symposium a hostile "gathering".
  • A line means two of them appear in the same memo, and a thicker line means they do so in more memos.
  • Red lines are pairings that never happened before 1983.

What it shows

Before 1983 the memos stick to three themes that turn up together. After the symposium went ahead despite the KGB's countermeasures, four new themes appear, among them a quoted "artificial famine" and the hostile "gathering", and sixteen new pairings link them to the old ones. The memos didn't just get more numerous. Their way of talking rewired.

Why it matters

Lotman's explosion is a sudden change in what can be said, not more of the same said louder. This figure is the dissertation's way of dating and counting such a change inside one institution: the outside world's story won a round in 1983, and the secret register reorganised itself in response. The before side rests on only two memos, so the journal treats this as a candidate result until more early memos are found. Read it alongside L1, which shows what crossed outward, and L2, which counts the wording habits.

Potential for the bigger picture

The most exciting idea on the site and the most starved of data. Two memos before 1983 are too few to call anything a rewiring with confidence, since one extra early memo could move many of the "new" pairings onto the before side. That's a limit of the archive, not a flaw in the idea, and the journal already treats the result as a candidate.

What would help: more pre-1983 memos from the same fond, so the two periods are closer in size; a comparison that adjusts for the number of memos, for instance by drawing many two-memo samples from the after period and counting how often that many new pairings appear by chance; and a check that the operators from the 1955 manual persist while the neighbourhoods change, which the colloquium notes suggest as a slow clock. If the rewiring survives those, it becomes the dissertation's clearest dated instance of Lotman's explosion.
